# Combat UI

After the death camera fall, Q/E cycles living teammates in first person. The owner publishes its rendered camera world position/rotation and vertical FOV from LateUpdate after prediction presentation, including pitch, stance transitions, recoil and ADS/sprint zoom. Orientation is transmitted as a quaternion derived from the rendered camera basis. The spectator plays that pose on a delayed source-time interpolation timeline, without a chase offset or camera wall pull-in. Render-only mesh suppression hides the observed actor's body, third-person weapons and nameplate while leaving simulation and colliders running.

SpectatorRules selects eligible teammates. MultiplayerSession.Spectating relays immutable SpectatorViewState snapshots on channel 29 to eliminated teammates only. Client identity comes from the transport; the host checks life, sequence, finite/bounded values and proximity to the authoritative actor. These snapshots never influence movement, damage or inventory. Dead/missing/stale targets fall back to another eligible teammate or the waiting view. Bot views derive from authoritative eye/aim and equipment because bots have no rendered client screen.

PlayerController owns camera and first-person weapon presentation, with replicated hand offsets, fire/reload states, FOV and animation triggers. It keeps gameplay loadout/resources separate and restores the local camera, FOV, hands and body visibility on respawn or target loss. PlayerHud mirrors teammate weapon, ammo, health, armour, flash, spread and currency; GameModeHud uses the observed teammate for objective and supply presentation.

This is replicated gameplay POV, not video streaming: camera samples arrive at spectatorUpdatesPerSecond (with network latency), skeletal animations run on the spectator, and personal menus/settings, hit-confirmation cues and progression overlays are not screen-captured. Different viewport aspect ratios and graphics settings can differ. Protocol 19 requires all participants to use the updated scripts.

The buy menu puts wallet, buy window and transaction feedback above equipment. Cards show price, owned/carried quantity, affordability shortfall, capacity limits and remaining balance after purchase. Primary purchases explicitly replace the previous primary. Throwable selection is separate from purchase. The menu refreshes when quantities, ownership, objective interaction or buy time change. EconomyRules supplies presentation eligibility; MultiplayerSession guards local requests and retains authoritative host transaction validation. Unavailable cards leave keyboard traversal and pointer input; guards also reject stale queued clicks.

Validation: build CoD.Scripts.csproj; run Tests/CoD.GameplayTests.csproj with --economy-grenades and Tests/Settings/Settings.Tests.csproj. Use PlutoGERmlUiInterfaceScaleTests on Vulkan/OpenGL. Live multiplayer review should cover teammate death/disconnect/team changes, no surviving teammates, respawn, menu input capture, and inventory scrolling at high interface scales. Existing native scale captures cover HUD/settings, not buy-menu gameplay state.

## Editor authoring

First-person spectator POV uses the owning player's authored camera and PlayerController ECS settings. Tune the normal camera, stance heights, ADS FOV and weapon hand bindings on that player; spectators receive the resulting rendered pose. The old spectator-only position/rotation/collision overrides were removed because they altered the owner's POV. MultiplayerSession.spectatorUpdatesPerSecond controls human camera snapshot cadence (default 60, bounded to 1-60 Hz), independently of world-state replication. MultiplayerSession.spectatorInterpolationDelay controls playback delay (default 0.1 seconds, bounded to 0-0.25). Enemy/RemotePlayer prefabs expose spectatorBodyMesh for render-only hiding.

Dedicated-server regression: `dotnet run --project Tests/NetworkEconomy/CoD.NetworkEconomyTests.csproj -c Release -- <PlutoGEServer.exe> <CoDplutoproject.plutoproject> --spectator` starts three clients, eliminates an observer through server-owned combat, verifies camera/hand/HUD values and transport-stamped identity, checks living/enemy privacy, and verifies delivery stops on respawn.

## Spectator pose and body lifecycle

PlayerController reapplies the sampled spectator world pose in LateUpdate, after physics render extrapolation and built-in camera rigs, so the eliminated local player's parent transform cannot introduce death-camera roll or lower the POV. Body bindings are resolved repeatedly until the proxy script is ready and rebound when an existing peer receives a replacement proxy. EnemySoldierBot suppresses the authored body/weapon mesh components while observed, captures each original enabled state once, and restores those states on target change or respawn. Physics, animation and colliders remain active.

Bot `EnemySoldierBot.spectatorEyeHeight` is editor-controlled, measured above its capsule-centre root (default `0.5`, matching the human camera convention). Camera origin is independent of CombatTargeting.AimPoint, which is intentionally a chest-level target. Human camera height and orientation continue to come directly from the owner's rendered camera.

Focused lifecycle regression: `dotnet run --project Tests/SpectatorPresentation/SpectatorPresentation.Tests.csproj -c Release`. It exercises the real managed controller against a native API adapter, covering render-timeline pose correction, delayed body references, same-peer proxy replacement and original visibility restoration. Visual gameplay confirmation still requires the running game.

## Orientation and smooth playback

World Euler `(pitch, yaw, 0)` is not a level-horizon camera in the engine's Rx * Ry * Rz convention. Bot camera orientation composes heading before camera-local pitch through a quaternion. Human orientation comes from the rendered forward/right basis. SpectatorCameraMath converts quaternion orientation to engine Euler only at the camera API boundary, preserving intentional human camera roll while eliminating yaw-dependent tilt. Interpolated bot cameras explicitly retain an upright horizon when pitch and yaw change together. Compass heading comes from the quaternion's forward direction rather than a folded Euler yaw.

SpectatorViewBuffer interpolates camera position, quaternion orientation, FOV and same-weapon hand poses using sender timestamps. The clock is anchored separately for each source. The default 100 ms playback delay absorbs irregular packet arrival, especially the bots' 20 Hz world snapshots. Presentation runs once in LateUpdate, with no competing OnUpdate writes. Discrete weapon/HUD events follow the source timeline. Teleports, life changes and target changes do not blend through the map; missing packets hold the latest pose and the existing stale-target timeout still applies. The buffer is bounded, drops duplicate/out-of-order data, and does not extrapolate through walls. HUD updates compare HUD fields instead of rebuilding for each interpolated camera position.

The focused gameplay tests include yaw sweeps with pitch, camera basis/engine XYZ round trips, quaternion yaw wrap, 20 Hz jittered arrivals rendered at 60 Hz, teleport resets and new-life resets.
